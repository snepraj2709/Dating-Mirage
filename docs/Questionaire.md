## Transition From Step 1 to Step 2

The transition from Step 1 to Step 2 should feel like a supportive, non-judgmental reality check with a message:

> *"Aspirations are beautiful, but patterns live in our history. Let’s look at your last 2–3 relationships or situationships. No judgment, just data."*

Focus more to capture information that Step 1 entirely missed (like subconscious compromises, trauma responses, and hidden trade-offs), Step 2 must focus on **historical outcomes and behavioral side effects** rather than preferences.

## Step 4: The Mirror Analysis

### The "Blind Spot" Delta (Johari Window Model) Algorithm

Instead of just calculating a single, flat "Gap," it calculates **Awareness**.

By triangulating the three inputs—what the user *wants*, what they *admit* to choosing, and what their friends *actually* see—we map every single dimension onto a 2D plane. It mathematically separates **Self-Sabotage** (patterns they know they have but can't break) from **Delusion** (patterns they are completely blind to).

The Johari Window Model (Experiment 3) is the most psychologically revealing algorithm you can build for this product. Instead of just calculating a single, flat "Gap," this engine calculates Awareness.

By triangulating the three inputs—what the user wants, what they admit to choosing, and what their friends actually see—we map every single dimension onto a 2D plane. It mathematically separates Self-Sabotage (patterns they know they have but can't break) from Delusion (patterns they are completely blind to).

Here is the deep-dive architectural blueprint for the Johari Window backend algorithm across your 8 dimensions.

#### 1. The Vector Definitions

For every user, the system generates three 8-dimensional arrays (vectors) based on the $1.0$ to $10.0$ scales established in the questionnaires.

Let $d$ represent one of the 8 dimensions: $\{CON, INT, AUT, VAL, GOC, VUL, REA, RWO\}$.

| Vector | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Ideal Vector ($I_d$) | Step 1 Data (Who I say I want). |
| Actual Vector ($A_d$) | Step 2 Data (Who I admit I choose). |
| Social Vector ($S_d$) | Friend Data (The aggregated average of what friends notice). |

#### 2. The Core Algorithm Equations

For every single dimension, the algorithm calculates two distinct Deltas.

##### Equation 1: The Conscious Gap (Self-Sabotage)

This measures the distance between what the user aspires to and what they admit they actually do in reality.

$$ \Delta_{Conscious, d} = | I_d - A_d | $$

**High Score meaning:** "I know I want peace, but I admit I keep swiping on chaos."

##### Equation 2: The Blind Spot (Delusion)

This measures the distance between the user’s self-reported reality and the reality their friends observe. This is where the product delivers its "shock value."

$$ \Delta_{Blind, d} = | A_d - S_d | $$

**High Score meaning:** "I think I'm handling the situationship well, but my friends know I am spiraling."

##### Equation 3: Total Dimension Severity Score

To determine which of the 8 dimensions should be highlighted on the user's final "Dating Mirror Card," we calculate the Euclidean distance of both deltas to find the total magnitude of the user's contradiction. Since the maximum possible delta on a 1–10 scale is 9, the maximum distance is $\sqrt{9^2 + 9^2} \approx 12.72$.

$$ Severity_d = \left( \frac{\sqrt{(\Delta_{Conscious, d})^2 + (\Delta_{Blind, d})^2}}{12.72} \right) \times 100 $$

The algorithm sorts the 8 dimensions by their $Severity$ score and selects the Top 2 highest scores to build the user's final shareable card.

### The 2x2 Matrix Output: Categorizing the Gap

Once the deltas are calculated for a dimension, the algorithm plots the data on a 2x2 matrix. The threshold for a "High" delta is $> 3.0$. This dictates the exact flavor of the copy shown to the user.
